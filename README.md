During Q2, I established automation baselines across squads, identified key gaps, and worked with teams to define practical improvement plans aligned with the objective of reducing dependency on manual testing.

A key focus area was the Inferno squad, which had no meaningful automation at the start of the quarter. Working closely with the squad alongside ongoing BAU commitments, we successfully increased automation coverage from 0% to approximately 30%. In parallel, additional regression scenarios were identified to further expand automation coverage in subsequent quarters.

In the Starfighter squad, I partnered with the team to assess the regression suite and establish a structured automation roadmap. As a result, 42% of regression scenarios have now been identified for automation, with implementation planned for completion during Q3. While progress was impacted by several initiatives being cancelled during the testing phase due to changing business priorities, the groundwork established during Q2 positions the squad to accelerate automation delivery once priorities stabilise.

To support the capability’s long-term automation strategy, I encouraged engineers working with the legacy Java-based automation framework in the Employee Experience squad to develop TypeScript skills and adopt the new monorepo-based automation framework for new initiatives such as Provide Docs. This helps align future automation efforts with the organisation’s strategic direction and reduces investment in legacy tooling.

I also ensured appropriate knowledge transfer and mentoring were provided to QA engineers transitioning from predominantly manual testing into automation-focused roles. This has strengthened automation capability within the chapter and increased confidence in adopting modern automation practices across squads.
